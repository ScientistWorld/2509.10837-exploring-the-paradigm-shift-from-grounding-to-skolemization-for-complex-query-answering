# Gym scoring audit

The score responds to a substantial genuine improvement and to weakening, but the gym does **not reliably measure the paper's research contribution**. The substitute data removes two central difficulties: all 283 hidden queries are exactly answerable from the supplied graph, and removing negation changes the answer set of **zero of 38** hidden negated queries. The brute-force shortcut solves the test perfectly in under one second. Better performance here therefore does not establish better reasoning on the paper's incomplete-graph problem.

**Do better results score higher?** Yes for the substantial changes tested below. The reference has room above it: **no scored reference metric is at its intended bound**. Its eleven accuracy/trace metrics are below 1 and its reported size is 252.416k parameters, above 0. The random baseline is clearly below the reference.

The better variant keeps the LVSA architecture and parameter count. It pretrains entity/relation embeddings using every supplied observed triple and its inverse, uses uniform targets over true tails, and trains stages for 200/60/30 epochs. The reference's inverse embeddings are used during reasoning without direct inverse-query supervision. This variant uses only the supplied graph and training queries, opens no validation/test answer labels during training, and still predicts through the neural LVSA model. It is a genuine training improvement, rather than the graph-execution shortcuts below. The weaker variant uses 18/0/10 epochs instead of 18/18/10. Both rank movements exceed seed noise under both disclosed normalizations. Three times as much training alone gives a rank gain smaller than seed spread; that aggregate gain is inconclusive.

Rank change is relative to the **rerun hidden-test reference**, not a raw sum of metric units. Following the supplied rank definition, each term is `experiment_weight * abs(coefficient) * direction * (value - fixed_reference) / population_stddev`. Direction comes solely from `higher_is_better`; the negative sign of the size coefficient does not reverse it again. A is the 21 successful finite audit attempts; H uses the ten honest quality/reproduction attempts as normalization scales. All rows use the same scales within each column. The eventual future ranking cohort is unknown, so these are disclosed audit scores, not predictions of its numeric leaderboard. [summary.json](redteam/summary.json) records scales, cohorts, contributions, complete metrics and absolute scores against the fixed targets. Fixed targets themselves differ from the rerun reference (MRR 0.493147 versus 0.506149; size 251.904k versus 252.416k).

The reproduced method beats the runnable random baseline in the reported better direction. Removing the logical-axiom loss also lowers total MRR from 0.506149 to 0.485610 and negated-query MRR from 0.738841 to 0.585881, consistent in direction with the paper's negation-loss ablation. However, random ranking is much weaker than the paper's actual comparison methods, and the synthetic negative atom excludes no answers. This does not reproduce the paper's claimed advantage over CQD, BetaE, ConE, LMPNN or CLMPT. Their published numbers are static context; neither paper experiment is written by the hidden test or contributes a measured result.

The recognizable LVSA implementation includes complex binding, normalized bundling, differentiable Skolemization and the three-stage freeze/unfreeze curriculum. Material departures from [the local paper, Sections IV and VI](paper/2509.10837.md) are:

| Aspect | Gym reproduction versus paper |
|---|---|
| Data and scale | A generated 160-entity, four-relation graph with 3,000/500/700 train/valid/test queries replaces official FB15k, FB15k-237 and NELL995 splits. Only five query types are generated, without the paper's broader 14-type evaluation, new 4p/4i queries or fully unobservable benchmarks. |
| Initialization and optimization | Xavier initialization and sampled 1p training replace pretrained ComplEx embeddings. Dimension 64 and hidden width 128 replace the reported embedding dimension 1,000; Adam learning rate 0.005 replaces 0.0005. The gym fixes 18/18/10 epochs without reproducing the reported validation/grid-search protocol. |
| Neural modules | ReLU replaces LeakyReLU. A separate `mlp_n_atom` network handles repeated negation, rather than applying the same neural negator in the paper's double-negation constraint. |
| Training objectives | Entity prediction only, without the reported auxiliary relation prediction objective or the in-batch candidate cross-entropy term of Eq. 25. Cross-entropy always targets `answers[0]`, instead of representing the full correct-answer training distribution. Alpha and beta equal 1, as reported. |
| Query semantics | Synthetic `2in` contains two positive atoms and a third negative atom, corresponding to the paper's three-atom `3in` form. The implemented graph traversal covers the supplied anchored structures, without demonstrating the claimed arbitrary EFO1 coverage. |
| Accuracy evaluation | First correct answer per query, with truncated top-20 reference rankings, replaces filtered ranking of every non-trivial answer in Eqs. 23–24. Missing correct answers need not hurt if one answer ranks first. |
| Comparison and resources | Random baseline and one loss ablation replace trained paper baselines. No scored throughput or memory metric checks the paper's efficiency claim; the size metric trusts method-authored metadata. |

[Data diagnostics](redteam/data_checks.json) show that all 700 full-file queries, including all 283 hidden queries, have exactly their labeled answers in the supplied 1,114-triple graph. Negation changes zero of 93 full-file negated answer sets. Sixty-three hidden queries also occur in training. There are 166 hidden multi-answer queries and 715 labeled answers; the label-copy shortcut emits only 283 answers yet receives perfect MRR and Hits. The synthetic setting has neither missing-fact reasoning nor effective negation, and the scoring additionally discards answer coverage. These are failures of scientific task fidelity, despite useful ordering between the neural variants.

Normalization matters: the huge negative parameter count also inflates that metric's population spread. It does **not** produce an unlimited rank gain once included in the cohort. That is why its A gain is 0.449655, while its gain under fixed honest scales is enormous. The zero-size shortcut still improves rank under either finite normalization. No sign-of-coefficient or raw-unit shortcut was assumed.

**Scoring contract checks.** Ordinary empty, random and constant methods on unchanged data all rank below the reference despite zero parameters. That limited check passes. There is no hard task-success or constraint gate, however: selective trace omission and size claims increase rank without better answers; the constant-output ground-truth shortcut outranks the reference while reporting zero trace quality. The scalar sum cannot certify that the task and its constraints were met.

Failure handling does not satisfy the required worst-value contract. Empty rankings receive an MRR floor of `1/161` and zero Hits/traces, but omitting the sidecar omits the size metric entirely. Null metadata aborts instead of emitting worst values for every metric. The two paper experiments are absent for every method. Missing values/experiments are skipped by the stated rank rule. These runs establish omissions and errors, rather than an invented successful score for an aborted test; downstream treatment of a missing score file was not available to audit.

Method coverage also fails: `shortcut_hide_failure/run_result.json` records both prediction files on disk, whereas its hidden manifest and `results` record only the registered candidate. The no-argument test trusts the persisted train manifest first, and train result names second, before falling back to output discovery. Checking test names against train names therefore does not enforce discovery of all methods.

No baseline model, operating threshold or evaluator process was modified. This gym has no fixed trained detector/reference artifact to retrain; replacement of its fixed graph and truth covers that trust boundary. Direct reported accuracy/success/timing spoofing failed. Nonfinite and null outputs were tried and are distinguished from successful shortcuts.

**Execution and cleanup.** The 23 cases ran in two bundled CPU jobs, `11cee678-5fe` and `8aa850d0-5b2`, both exit 0. Every case invokes the exact no-argument `bash scripts/evaluate_test.sh` in a new subprocess after creating its candidate and public train manifest. All candidates depend only on persisted files: no background process, evaluator edit, import-path change or Python-environment tampering is involved, so a fresh evaluation job sees the same candidate inputs. Reference predictions are reused where only reporting/scoring changes, as requested. Null deliberately fails its individual hidden test and has a saved [failure excerpt](redteam/shortcut_null/failure_evidence.txt).

The runner snapshots and restores data, outputs, temporary manifests and scoring artifacts around each case. The temporary scheduler entrypoint and scheduler-created job directory are restored from the recorded base commit with git; compact job evidence is preserved in [job_records.json](redteam/job_records.json). Audit additions are confined to `redteam/` and this report, apart from the required final `action.yaml` done signal. Pre-existing harness changes listed in `redteam/initial_status.txt` are preserved. [validation.json](redteam/validation.json) records 23 runnable case scripts, 22 successful hidden tests, the deliberate null failure, source/shell validation, snapshot comparisons, and unchanged protected directories. Reproduction milestones are unchanged because this work audits the finished gym.

The next iteration should use nontrivial incomplete-graph queries with effective negation, preserve immutable evaluation truth/graph independently of scientist-writable data, score every correct answer and every required trace, derive resource costs independently, discover every submission, and emit complete finite worst scores on failure. Merely hiding the current answer lists would leave the deterministic-reconstruction and exhaustive-grounding shortcuts intact.
